## 答案与解析

**1.** A. Carbon dioxide, methane, water vapour  
　Explanation: Greenhouse gases that absorb and re-emit infrared radiation include CO₂, CH₄, water vapour, N₂O and CFCs. Oxygen and nitrogen make up 99% of the atmosphere, but they do not absorb infrared radiation and are not greenhouse gases.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section I(terms / metalanguage)

**2.** B. Extra warming caused by human increases in greenhouse gas concentrations  
　Explanation: The natural greenhouse effect keeps the Earth's average temperature at about 15°C rather than −18°C, and it is essential. 'Enhanced' refers to the additional greenhouse gases released by human activities, which strengthen that effect.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section I

**3.** C. Short-wave solar radiation passes through the atmosphere, is absorbed by the surface and re-emitted as long-wave radiation, which is then trapped  
　Explanation: Solar radiation, mainly short-wave, passes through the atmosphere to reach the Earth's surface; as the surface warms, it radiates long-wave (infrared) radiation, which is absorbed and re-emitted by greenhouse gases, with some returning to the surface.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section II(greenhouse effect)

**4.** D. The ocean absorbing dissolved carbon dioxide  
　Explanation: Carbon sinks, such as oceans, forests, and soil, absorb and store carbon, while sources like coal burning, deforestation, and ruminant animals release carbon.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section I

**5.** A. Thermal expansion of warming seawater and melting of land-based ice  
　Explanation: There are two main mechanisms: thermal expansion of seawater (accounting for about half of the contribution) and the melting of land-based ice (glaciers, Greenland, Antarctica) flowing into the oceans; note that 'sea ice melting' does not raise sea levels as it already floats on water.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section II(sea level rise)

**6.** B. Carbon dioxide dissolves in seawater forming carbonic acid, lowering pH  
　Explanation: The reaction CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid) releases H⁺, decreasing pH; since the Industrial Revolution, the pH of surface seawater has decreased by about 0.1 (an increase in acidity of around 30%).  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section II(ocean acidification)

**7.** C. Loss of the symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae) due to heat stress, exposing the white skeleton  
　Explanation: When water temperatures remain high, corals expel their symbiotic algae (zooxanthellae), losing their colour and primary source of energy, revealing their white skeletons; if high temperatures persist for too long, corals will die.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section II(ocean temperature and coral bleaching)

**8.** D. A cold front  
　Explanation: Cold fronts are represented by a triangle symbol (▲) and warm fronts by a semicircle symbol (◗), with the direction of the triangle or semicircle indicating the direction of the front's movement; isobars are shown as fine solid lines.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section I(synoptic charts)

**9.** A. Clear skies and settled weather  
　Explanation: High-pressure systems have sinking air, which is less likely to form clouds and rain, resulting in stable and clear weather; low-pressure systems have rising air, which easily forms clouds and precipitation.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section I(skills · synoptic charts)

**10.** B. Logical fallacy (cherry-picking)  
　Explanation: Cherry-picking involves selecting a short-term, local weather event to dispute long-term, global trends, but weather is not the same as climate: a single cold snap cannot overturn a 30-year warming trend.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section I(critical thinking · FLICC)

**11.** C. Fake experts  
　Explanation: Fake experts are used to create the illusion of 'expert controversy' by using people without relevant field credentials; judge them based on their research field and peer review record, not just their title.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section I(FLICC)

**12.** D. A positive feedback loop (albedo effect)  
　Explanation: High albedo ice reflects sunlight, but when it melts, the exposed dark sea surface absorbs more heat, leading to further warming and ice melting, which is a positive feedback loop.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section I(critical thinking / climate system)

**13.** A. There is a positive correlation, consistent with CO₂ driving warming  
　Explanation: Correlation is not causation, but where a positive correlation is consistent with physical mechanisms, the conclusion should be worded as 'consistent with'. Saying 'proven' is wrong, and so is saying the data means nothing.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section I(graph interpretation)

**14.** B. Higher sea level, larger storm surges and more intense East Coast Lows  
　Explanation: Sea level rise allows the same storm surge to act on a higher base, and the combined effects of storm surge, East Coast Lows, and increased exposure due to coastal development amplify the risk.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography(climate change and impacts on coastal environments)

**15.** Short-wave radiation from the Sun passes through the atmosphere and is absorbed by the Earth's surface, warming it. The surface re-emits this energy as longer-wave infrared radiation. Greenhouse gases (CO₂, methane, water vapour) absorb this infrared radiation and re-radiate it in all directions, including back towards the surface. This trapped energy raises the average surface temperature. Without this effect the Earth's average temperature would be about −18°C instead of about 15°C.  
　Explanation: The four steps that earn marks are: short-wave entry; surface absorption and long-wave emission; greenhouse gas absorption and re-radiation; and the resulting warming. Getting the order wrong, or leaving out 're-radiation back to the surface', loses marks.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section II(greenhouse effect)

**16.** The natural greenhouse effect is a process that has always operated: naturally occurring greenhouse gases trap some outgoing infrared radiation and keep the average surface temperature at about 15°C, which makes life possible. The enhanced greenhouse effect is the additional warming caused by human activities that increase greenhouse gas concentrations — burning fossil fuels (CO₂), agriculture and livestock (methane), and deforestation. The mechanism is identical; what differs is the extra quantity of greenhouse gases and therefore the extra trapped energy.  
　Explanation: The key is that the mechanism is the same but the quantities differ. Writing only that 'one is natural and the other is human-caused' will not earn full marks; you have to point out that the extra greenhouse gases retain more energy.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section II

**17.** First, thermal expansion: as seawater warms it expands and takes up more volume. Second, melting of land-based ice (glaciers, Greenland, Antarctica) adds water to the ocean. (Melting sea ice does not raise sea level because it already displaces its own weight.) Impacts: increased coastal erosion and shoreline retreat during storms; inundation of low-lying areas and infrastructure; saltwater intrusion into estuaries and coastal groundwater; loss of beaches and wetlands; greater damage from storm surges.  
　Explanation: The mechanisms must include two items (thermal expansion and land ice), and note that sea ice is not included; the impacts must be specifically related to the consequences for the Australian coast (erosion, inundation, saltwater, and wetland loss).  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section II(sea level rise)

**18.** The ocean absorbs about a quarter of human CO₂ emissions. Dissolved CO₂ reacts with water to form carbonic acid (CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃), which releases hydrogen ions and lowers the pH of seawater (ocean acidification). The extra hydrogen ions combine with carbonate ions, reducing the carbonate available to organisms. Corals, shellfish and plankton need carbonate to build calcium carbonate shells and skeletons, so their growth slows and shells can dissolve, weakening reef structures and food webs.  
　Explanation: The three steps are: absorption of CO₂, formation of carbonic acid and decrease in pH, and reduction of carbonate ions and inhibition of calcification; the final step must be related to the biological consequences (coral, shellfish, and plankton).  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section II(ocean acidification)

**19.** Corals live in symbiosis with zooxanthellae, algae that live in their tissue, give them their colour and supply most of their energy through photosynthesis. When water temperatures rise well above the summer average (or the coral is stressed by pollution or extreme low tides), the coral expels the zooxanthellae. The coral tissue becomes transparent, showing the white calcium carbonate skeleton — bleaching. If the stress is short and temperatures return to normal, the coral can take up algae again and recover over weeks to months. If the heat stress persists, the coral starves and dies, and recovery can take a decade or more.  
　Explanation: You have to explain the link that 'the symbiotic algae supply energy and colour', otherwise you cannot explain why the coral dies. The recovery conditions are relief from heat stress in time and good water quality.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section II(ocean temperature and coral bleaching)

**20.** Before the front arrives: warm, moist north-westerly winds ahead of the front, rising temperature and humidity, increasing cloud. As the cold front crosses: a narrow band of heavy rain, thunderstorms, a sharp temperature drop and a wind shift from north-west to south-west, with a rapid rise in pressure afterwards. After the front passes: cooler, drier air, clearing skies and settled weather as the high moves in, with cold nights.  
　Explanation: The trick is to read the chart in three stages: ahead of the front (warm and humid, pressure falling) → the crossing (heavy rain, cooling, a sudden change in wind direction) → behind the front (dry and cold, clearing, pressure rising). Every point you make must match an element of the chart.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section II(synoptic charts)

**21.** The two techniques are: (1) cherry-picking/oversimplification — 'climate has always changed' ignores the rate and the different driver (natural cycles such as orbital change operated over tens of thousands of years, whereas current warming tracks the added greenhouse gases and is far faster), and (2) conspiracy theory — claiming scientists are motivated by funding rather than evidence. Rebuttal: natural factors alone cannot reproduce the observed warming of the last century, particularly the pattern of stratospheric cooling alongside tropospheric warming, which is the fingerprint of greenhouse gases. Scientific claims should be assessed on evidence and mechanism, not on assumed motives; the funding accusation is not evidence.  
　Explanation: Marks come from two things: correctly identifying the two techniques, and giving evidence-based rebuttals. Simply criticising people without evidence scores nothing.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography Section I(critical thinking · FLICC)

**22.** Mitigation reduces the causes of climate change by cutting greenhouse gas emissions or increasing carbon sinks — for example building large-scale solar and wind generation and retiring coal-fired power, or protecting and restoring coastal wetlands as a carbon sink. Adaptation reduces vulnerability to the effects that can no longer be avoided — for example building sea walls and dune restoration programs at Collaroy-Narrabeen, or planning retreat zones and raising building floor levels.  
　Explanation: Mitigation reduces the cause of the problem — cutting emissions or increasing absorption — while adaptation reduces the impact, through protection or avoidance. Examples should be Australian ones, ideally from the coast.  
　出处：Yearly Study Guide 2026 · Geography(climate change responses)

**23.** C. That environmental change operates at different rates and scales  
　Explanation: The core concept is that environmental change has both time and spatial scales: a single storm can reshape a beach in hours, while dune migration takes thousands of years. That is why 'change' has to be described in terms of rate and scale.  
　出处：Term 3 · Lesson 1.1 Environmental Change(rate and scale of change)

**24.** D. An East Coast Low, bringing heavy rain and large erosion-driven waves  
　Explanation: Dense isobars mean a large pressure gradient, which equals strong winds; a closed low-pressure system with a cold front brings heavy rain, strong winds, and large waves to the east coast, which was the weather context for the severe erosion of the beach in 2016.  
　出处：Term 3 · Lesson 2.2 Understanding East Coast Lows(synoptic map)

**25.** A. Prevention — stopping change; mitigation — reducing impact; preparedness — planning response  
　Explanation: Prevention stops the problem from arising, mitigation reduces the impact of change, and preparedness plans the response to an event. The category a strategy belongs to decides the standard it is evaluated against, so do not mix them up.  
　出处：Term 3 · Lessons 3.1–3.2 Sustainable Management of Coastal Ecosystems

**26.** Tightly packed isobars mean a steep pressure gradient and therefore strong winds, which generate large waves and a storm surge that raises water levels at the shore. The cold front adds intense rainfall and runoff, so the combination of higher water and bigger waves produced a major storm cut and foredune erosion.  
　Explanation: The chain of mechanism is: dense isobars → strong winds → large waves and storm surge; cold front → heavy rain. When these coincide with high tide, the erosion is destructive. Saying only 'the wind was strong' will not earn full marks.  
　出处：Term 3 · Lesson 2.2 Understanding East Coast Lows

**27.** (a) Prevention — it removes the exposure before damage can occur. (b) Mitigation — it reduces the impact of wave energy on assets that already exist. (c) Preparedness — it plans the response for when an event happens.  
　Explanation: The basis for judgement is 'before, during, or after the change': prevention avoids exposure, mitigation reduces damage to existing assets, and preparedness arranges for responses during events.  
　出处：Term 3 · Lessons 3.1–3.2 Sustainable Management of Coastal Ecosystems

**28.** The protection argument is valid in the short term: the wall prevents wave attack on the houses immediately behind it. The criticism is also valid: a vertical wall reflects wave energy, removes sediment from the beach budget and can increase erosion at the ends of the wall, so the beach in front narrows and the problem shifts along the coast. The decision therefore trades protection of private assets against the loss of public beach.  
　Explanation: Evaluation questions need both sides shown to be valid, with the cost of the decision explained: short-term protection works but carries real coastal-process costs. Arguing only one side will not score high marks.  
　出处：Term 3 · Lesson 3.3 The Controversial Seawall(stakeholder perspectives)
