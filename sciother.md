## Answers and explanations

**1.** A. Visible light and infrared  
  Explanation: The main drivers of Earth's processes that reach the surface are visible light and infrared radiation: visible light provides energy for photosynthesis, and infrared radiation heats the hydrosphere, driving the water cycle. X-rays and microwaves are mostly absorbed by the atmosphere and do not reach the surface.  
  Source: Term 1 · Earth's Energy – Earth.pdf(Topic 6 Earth · Solar radiation)

**2.** B. Lithosphere  
  Explanation: The lithosphere = crust + upper mantle, approximately 100 km thick. The atmosphere is a layer of gas, the biosphere is the range of biological survival, and the hydrosphere is the solid/liquid/gaseous water on the surface.  
  Source: Term 1 · Earth's Energy – Revision Booklet(Topic 6 Earth · Terminology)

**3.** C. Albedo decreases, so more solar radiation is absorbed and the temperature rises further.  
  Explanation: As ice melts it exposes dark seawater, so the albedo falls, more solar radiation is absorbed and the temperature rises, which melts even more ice: a positive feedback loop.  
  Source: Term 1 · Earth's Energy – STUDENT 5. Earth.docx(Practical 2 Albedo Effect)

**4.** Infrared radiation from the Sun warms the hydrosphere, so liquid water evaporates into water vapour. The vapour rises and cools, condenses into clouds, and eventually falls back to the surface as precipitation such as rain, hail or snow.  
  Explanation: The water cycle is driven by solar infrared radiation heating the hydrosphere: evaporation → rising and cooling → condensation → precipitation, which returns water to the oceans, lakes and rivers.  
  Source: Term 1 · Earth's Energy – Earth.pdf(Topic 6 · The Water Cycle)

**5.** D. A section of DNA that codes for a protein  
  Explanation: A gene is a segment of DNA that encodes a protein. A chromosome is a form of DNA organisation (23 pairs in humans), and DNA is composed of nucleotides; the three are not at the same level.  
  Source: Term 1 · Genetics – Revision Booklet(Topic 8 Genetics · Terminology)

**6.** A. TCA ATG GAC  
  Explanation: Complementary DNA base pairing is A–T and G–C, so AGT pairs with TCA, TAC with ATG and CTG with GAC. Any option containing U is an mRNA sequence, not a complementary DNA strand.  
  Source: Term 1 · Genetics – 2020 Year 10 Yearly Examination Answers(Part D Q3)

**7.** B. 20%  
  Explanation: Given A=T, T also makes up 30%, totaling 60% for both, and the remaining 40% is split evenly between G and C, so G makes up 20%.  
  Source: Term 1 · Genetics – 2020 Year 10 Yearly Examination Answers (Part D Q4) + base-pairing rules

**8.** Mitosis produces two genetically identical diploid daughter cells and is used for growth and repair of body cells. Meiosis produces four genetically different haploid gametes, each with half the chromosome number of the parent cell.  
  Explanation: Mitosis produces 2 diploid daughter cells for growth and repair, while meiosis produces 4 haploid gametes for sexual reproduction (23+23=46 to restore diploidy).  
  Source: Term 1 · Genetics – Genetics.pdf(Knowledge 2 · Compare mitosis and meiosis)

**9.** C. The reading frame shifts, so every codon after that point may code for a different amino acid.  
  Explanation: Codons are read in groups of three bases, so inserting a single base shifts every codon after it (a frameshift) and usually changes the protein sequence drastically. Only inserting a multiple of three bases affects just one amino acid.  
  Source: Term 1 · Genetics – Genetics.pdf(Knowledge 3 · Frameshift - Insertion)

**10.** D. 2/3  
  Explanation: For Tt × Tt the ratio is 1TT : 2Tt : 1tt, which is 3 : 1 tall to short. Because the question asks for the ratio within the tall plants (3 parts), the heterozygous Tt makes up 2/3 of them.  
  Source: Term 1 · Genetics – Half Yearly Revision.pdf (Punnett squares: genotype and phenotype ratios)

**11.** A. 15 m/s  
  Explanation: The speed is v = d/t = 300 m / 20 s = 15 m/s, and 0.067 m/s is the result of inverting the formula (20/300), while 320 m/s is the sum of the two numbers.  
  Source: Term 1 · Motion – Speed and Velocity Problem Solving Jan 10(v = d/t)

**12.** B. Displacement  
  Explanation: Displacement has both magnitude and direction, and is a vector; distance, speed, and time have magnitude only, and are scalars.  
  Source: Term 1 · Motion – Half Yearly Revision.pdf(Scalar vs Vector)

**13.** C. 67 m/s  
  Explanation: The final velocity is v_f = v_i + at = 25 + 7.0 × 6.0 = 25 + 42 = 67 m/s. The 42 m/s option is only the change in velocity, from forgetting to add the initial velocity, and 175 m/s comes from 25 × 7, the wrong operation.  
  Source: Term 1 · Motion – Acceleration Problems (Practice) (Question 10)

**14.** D. The acceleration  
  Explanation: The gradient of a velocity–time graph is Δv/Δt, which is acceleration, while the area under the line represents displacement — a common point of confusion (area vs gradient).  
  Source: Term 1 · Motion – SG 1.1 Study Guide(velocity–time graph)

**15.** a = (v_f − v_i)/t = (0 − 20)/5.0 = −4.0 m/s², so the car decelerates at 4.0 m/s². F = ma = 1200 × (−4.0) = −4800 N, that is, a net force of 4800 N opposite to the car's motion.  
  Explanation: First use the acceleration formula — final velocity minus initial velocity, divided by time, which gives a negative value for deceleration — then substitute into F = ma. The net force is 4800 N, in the opposite direction to the motion.  
  Source: Term 1 · Motion – Half Yearly Revision.pdf (DESA: average acceleration and F = ma)

**16.** A. Hydrogen  
  Explanation: Acid + metal gives a salt and hydrogen gas, and the hydrogen can be tested with a lit wooden splint (the pop test). Acid + carbonate gives CO₂, which is tested with limewater.  
  Source: Term 2 · Reactive Chemistry – Term 3 Reactive Chemistry Week 5 Answers(Acid-Metal Reaction)

**17.** This is a neutralisation reaction. The word equation is: hydrochloric acid + sodium hydroxide → sodium chloride + water.  
  Explanation: Acid + base → salt + water: a neutralisation reaction. The metal part of the salt comes from the base (Na⁺) and the non-metal part from the acid (Cl⁻), giving sodium chloride.  
  Source: Term 2 · Reactive Chemistry – Term 3 Reactive Chemistry Week 7 Answers(Neutralisation Reaction)

**18.** B. Particles gain kinetic energy, so more collisions succeed.  
  Explanation: The collision theory requires particles to collide with sufficient force and in the correct direction.  
  Source: Term 2 · Reactive Chemistry – 2020 Year 10 Yearly Examination Answers (Part A Q7 — collision theory)

**19.** copper carbonate + nitric acid → copper nitrate + water + carbon dioxide. The salt formed is copper nitrate. Bubble the gas through limewater; if the limewater turns milky (chalky white), the gas is carbon dioxide.  
  Explanation: Acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide; the second part of the salt's name comes from the acid (nitric acid → nitrate), so it's copper nitrate.  
  Source: Term 2 · Reactive Chemistry – Term 3 Reactive Chemistry Week 7 Answers(balanced equations / acid-carbonate)

**20.** C. 2, 6, 2, 3  
  Explanation: 2Al + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂: the number of atoms on the left side is Al 2, H 6, Cl 6, and on the right side is Al 2, Cl 6, H 6, making them equal.  
  Source: Term 2 · Reactive Chemistry – Term 3 Reactive Chemistry Week 7 Answers(Balanced Equations part 2)

**21.** D. The number of neutrons  
  Explanation: Isotopes refer to atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.  
  Source: Term 2 · Nuclear Reactions – atomic_models_interactive_v2.html(Isotopes)

**22.** A. Nuclear fusion  
  Explanation: The sun's core is about 15 million °C, where hydrogen nuclei fuse into helium nuclei, releasing enormous energy, which is the source of the sun's radiation.  
  Source: Term 2 · Nuclear Reactions – The Universe 2020.pptx (Main Sequence Star) + Revision Booklet Topic 6 (definition of solar radiation)

**23.** While an organism is alive it continuously takes in small amounts of radioactive carbon dioxide from the air. When it dies the intake stops, so the radioactive carbon slowly decays into the non-radioactive form. Measuring how much radioactive carbon is left in the remains therefore shows how long ago the organism died.  
  Explanation: Living things constantly take in radioactive carbon; after death, the intake stops, and the remaining radioactive carbon decays at a fixed rate, allowing us to calculate the time of death.  
  Source: Term 2 · Nuclear Reactions – Year 10 - Evolution.pptx(Techniques used to date fossils · Absolute dating)

**24.** B. The enhanced greenhouse effect is the additional warming caused by human activities releasing abnormally high amounts of greenhouse gases.  
  Explanation: The natural greenhouse effect is the absorption of infrared radiation by greenhouse gases in the atmosphere, keeping the Earth's temperature relatively stable.  
  Source: Term 3 · Climate Science – Term 3 Earth Week 1 Answers(natural vs enhanced greenhouse effect)

**25.** C. Carbon dioxide  
  Explanation: The answer is carbon dioxide, as methane is combustible and nitrogen and argon are not the main greenhouse gases or products.  
  Source: Term 3 · Climate Science – 2020 Year 10 Yearly Examination Answers(Part B Q2)

**26.** D. Painting roofs white to mimic the albedo effect  
  Explanation: Mitigation involves reducing emissions and stabilising atmospheric greenhouse gas concentrations, while adaptation involves reducing social vulnerability to the impacts that are already happening.  
  Source: Term 3 · Climate Science – Revision Booklet(Topic 6 · Mitigation / Adaptation strategies)

**27.** A. The hydrogen ions compete with the shells for carbonate ions, so shellfish cannot build their shells.  
  Explanation: An excess of H⁺ ions will combine with carbonate ions to form bicarbonate, competing with shell formation for carbonate ions, making it harder for shells to form.  
  Source: Term 3 · Climate Science – Term 3 Earth Week 2 Answers(Ocean Acidification)

**28.** Frozen soils store large amounts of organic carbon and methane. When the permafrost thaws, microbes break this material down and release carbon dioxide and methane, which are both greenhouse gases. These extra gases trap more heat, which thaws even more permafrost, so the warming reinforces itself.  
  Explanation: The chain reaction is: permafrost thawing → microbial decomposition → release of CO₂ and methane → enhanced greenhouse effect → warming → more permafrost thawing, creating a positive feedback loop.  
  Source: Term 3 · Climate Science – SCNSW10CH00393.pdf (Global systems · permafrost) + Revision Booklet (permafrost methane)

**29.** B. Hexane  
  Explanation: The prefix hex- indicates 6 carbon atoms, and the suffix -ane indicates an alkane, so it's hexane.  
  Source: Term 3 · Organic Chemistry – KATZ - WEEK 3 LESSONS(Lesson 3 Naming Organic Compounds)

**30.** C. CnH2n+2  
  Explanation: The general formula for alkanes is CnH2n+2 (e.g. C5H12 when n=5), while alkenes are CnH2n and alkynes are CnH2n−2, with the last being alcohol classes (containing the -OH functional group).  
  Source: Term 3 · Organic Chemistry – Year 10 Organic Chemistry Homework Worksheet(Part C Q6)

**31.** Crude oil is heated so that most of it vaporises, and the vapours rise up the fractionating column, which is hot at the bottom and cooler at the top. Each fraction condenses at the level where the temperature falls below its boiling point. Smaller molecules have lower boiling points, so they rise further and are collected near the top.  
  Explanation: Distillation uses the difference in boiling points. The temperature inside the tower falls from bottom to top, so each component condenses at the height where the temperature drops below its boiling point. Smaller molecules have lower boiling points, rise higher and are drawn off near the top (LPG, gasoline), while larger molecules stay at the bottom (asphalt).  
  Source: Term 3 · Organic Chemistry – KATZ - WEEK 3 LESSONS(Lesson 2 Fractional Distillation)

**32.** D. 2-methylbutane  
  Explanation: The longest carbon chain has 4 carbon atoms (butane), with a methyl branch attached to the 2nd carbon; numbering must start from the end closest to the branch to get the smallest possible position number, so it's 2-methylbutane, and 3-methylbutane is an incorrect name for the same molecule numbered from the other end.  
  Source: Term 3 · Organic Chemistry – Year 10 Organic Chemistry Homework Worksheet(Part G Q12)

**33.** A. No, because alkanes follow CnH2n+2, which for five carbons is C₅H₁₂ and not C₅H₁₀.  
  Explanation: Substituting n=5 into the alkane formula CnH2n+2 gives C5H12; C5H10 has two fewer hydrogens, matching CnH2n, which is an alkene (or cycloalkane), and can't be a straight-chain alkane.  
  Source: Term 3 · Organic Chemistry – Year 10 Organic Chemistry Homework Worksheet(Part N Q32)
