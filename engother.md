## 答案与解析

**1.** A. Prejudice  
　Explanation: The original definition of prejudice in the Term 3 vocabulary list is 'a preconceived opinion that is not based on reason or experience'; empathy is about understanding and sharing others' feelings, ethics refers to a system of moral principles, and justice refers to fairness.  
　出处：Term 3 · Justice, Ethics and Morality — Key Definitions glossary (10ENGA)

**2.** B. Understanding and sharing the feelings of another person  
　Explanation: Empathy is defined in the vocabulary list as 'understanding and sharing the feelings of others'; the other items on the list are ethics, justice, and racism, which need to be distinguished from one another.  
　出处：Term 3 · JEM Key Definitions (10ENGA / LEEDER 10ENGB Homepage)

**3.** C. The deep emotional suffering and human tragedy caused by conflict  
　Explanation: The term 'pathos of war' refers to the deep emotional pain and human tragedy, such as grief, loss, and devastation, that result from conflict, rather than strategic, political or patriotic aspects.  
　出处：Term 1 · Pathos of War — All Quiet on the Western Front, introductory definition (10ENGR)

**4.** D. That the war has aged them psychologically, so they no longer feel young  
　Explanation: The continuous questioning and the phrase 'We are old now' create a paradox, highlighting that the characters' youth has been destroyed by war, not their age, and that the answer should focus on the techniques of 'rhetorical question + contrast'.  
　出处：Term 2 · AQWF essay extract — 'the impact of war on a soldier's identity' (Glover 10ENGY)

**5.** A. Emphasise how pointless his education now seems  
　Explanation: The rhetorical question juxtaposes 'being good at maths' with 'dying', implying that it's useless, which amplifies the absurdity of war and the waste of life, a typical example of pathos.  
　出处：Term 2 · AQWF essay extract — Leer's death passage (Glover 10ENGY)

**6.** B. Ideas of honour, glory, duty and patriotic heroism  
　Explanation: Traditional notions of war refer to the romanticized ideas of honour, glory, duty, and heroism; Remarque counters this narrative with the harsh realities of blood, fear, and meaningless death.  
　出处：Term 1 · AQWF additional practice questions (LEEDER 10ENGB Homepage)

**7.** 1 mark — he replaces heroic language with clinical bodily detail (rats, dysentery, amputations, the sounds of dying men).
1 mark — first-person narration through Paul makes the damage personal rather than statistical.
1 mark — the ironic gap between the schoolmaster's patriotic speeches and the soldiers' reality undermines the rhetoric of honour and duty.
1 mark — the effect is to make the reader experience war as physical and psychological damage rather than as glory.  
　Explanation: Four key points include the use of vivid details about bodies, diseases, and corpses, personalizing loss through first-person narrative, contrasting school patriotic speeches with reality, and focusing on the reader's experience of 'harm' rather than 'glory', with at least one literary device such as imagery, irony, or narrative perspective.  
　出处：Term 1 · The Pathos of War — 'Romanticism of War' lesson and AQWF novel study (LEEDER 10ENGB)

**8.** C. 'The Road Not Taken'  
　Explanation: The core situation of The Road Not Taken is about choice and a fork in the road, while Mending Wall is about neighbours repairing a wall, Birches is about childhood and escape, and Stopping by Woods is about stopping on a snowy night.  
　出处：Term 2 · Robert Frost Poetry — The Road Not Taken, poem and questions (LEEDER 10ENGB Homepage)

**9.** D. 'Mending Wall'  
　Explanation: The core situation of Mending Wall is about two families jointly repairing a stone wall every spring, using the wall to discuss boundaries, tradition, and the phrase 'Good fences make good neighbours'.  
　出处：Term 2 · Robert Frost Poetry — Mending Wall, poem and questions (LEEDER 10ENGB Homepage)

**10.** A. A traveller lingers in beautiful woods but remembers his promises  
　Explanation: The material highlights that the core of the poem is about obligation and responsibility, as the traveller is drawn to the snowy landscape but remembers 'promises to keep' and must continue their journey, with Birches corresponding to childhood and escape, Mending Wall to neighbourly boundaries, and The Road Not Taken to choice.  
　出处：Year 10 English 2026 — YEAR 10 EXAM STUDY MATERIALS (Frost imaginative prompts)

**11.** 1 mark — borrow the situation, not the plot: keep the isolated setting (a snow-filled wood, a stone wall at dusk) but invent a new persona.
1 mark — give the character a specific voice and a memory that explains their solitude.
1 mark — use one recurring image and controlled sentence length to create the reflective tone.
1 mark — organise the piece with a flashback that turns the reflection into a decision or a changed understanding.
1 mark — end on that change rather than on a summary of Frost's lines, so the piece is a new story rather than a retelling.  
　Explanation: The task requires using Frost's language features, conventions, and themes to create something new, not just retelling, with five key points: borrowing situations not plots, giving characters a voice and memory, using single images and sentence structure to control pace, using flashbacks to create a twist, and focusing on 'transformation' rather than summarizing the original poem.  
　出处：Term 2 · Frost imaginative task — 'construct an imaginative piece that deals with solitude and reflection' (Glover 10ENGY)

**12.** B. Historical images, protest poetry and speech extracts  
　Explanation: The material lists three types of texts: historical images, protest and civil rights poetry, and excerpts from civil rights figures' speeches, each requiring different analytical terms, which should be practised separately.  
　出处：Term 3 · Reading Section (15 marks) — task outline (10ENGA)

**13.** C. Defiant pride and a refusal to be diminished by oppression  
　Explanation: The poem expresses resistance and dignity through the repeated phrase 'I rise', belonging to the category of resistance in representations of injustice, discrimination, and resistance, rather than resignation or nostalgia.  
　出处：Term 3 · 'Still I Rise' poem and questions (10ENGL TERM THREE HOMEPAGE)

**14.** 1 mark — repetition of an assertive phrase works like a refrain, turning a private feeling into a public chant.
1 mark — the repeated assertion makes the speaker's defiance sound unstoppable, which is itself an argument against oppression.
1 mark — direct address ('you') pulls the reader or the oppressor into the poem, so the audience cannot remain neutral.
1 mark — combined with a rising tone, the poem turns testimony into a demand for recognition.  
　Explanation: Four key points include the use of repetition as a refrain, turning private emotions into public slogans, creating an 'irrepressible' tone, directly addressing the reader or oppressor, and turning testimony into demand, with analysis focusing on the effect on the reader, rather than just counting literary devices.  
　出处：Term 3 · Poem Techniques — 'Still I Rise' / 'Caged Bird' / 'They Took Me from My Mother' reading practice
